Stage 1 of 5

The question we are answering

“Which districts of Pakistan were hit hardest by the 2022 floods — and did relief reach the places that needed it most?”

To answer it honestly, we break it into three smaller questions:

  1. Where was the damage worst? — measured by satellite: how much land went under water (km²), and how many people lived in the flooded area.
  2. Where did relief go? — from 91,094 real relief-activity records: how many people were reached, in which district, by whom.
  3. Where is the gap? — districts with heavy flooding but little or no recorded relief.

Headline numbers (from the data itself)

31.3Mpeople lived in flooded areas (UNOSAT, Aug 2022)
83,557 km²maximum land under water — larger than Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
160districts measured by satellite
133districts with recorded relief work
Plain words: about 1 in every 7 people in Pakistan lived in an area the satellites saw under water. Sindh was hit by far the worst, then Punjab.

What this analysis covers

  • Time: floods June–November 2022 (satellite windows), relief work August 2022–December 2023.
  • Place: all of Pakistan, district by district.
  • Method: read the raw files → clean the mess → explore with charts → write findings. Every step is shown, including the cleaning most projects hide.

What changed: province rows removed (used as labels), junk/footnote rows dropped, Islamabad duplicate removed, numbers rounded, one row per district.

Decisions we had to make (written down, not hidden)

  • “Peak” value: the satellite file has 9 overlapping time windows. We took each district’s maximum flooded area and exposed population across the windows and call it the “peak”. Documented — not hidden.
  • Joining the two files: district names didn’t match (“CENTRAL KARACHI” vs “Central Karachi”, “Shaheed Benazirabad” vs “Shaheed Benazir Abad”). We built a documented alias map; 133 districts matched.
  • Missing numbers were left blank, never filled with 0 — blank means “not measured”.
  • Coverage = beneficiaries reached ÷ people exposed. It can exceed 100% because one person can be helped by several activities and agencies report their own numbers. We say so instead of hiding it.

Stage 5 of 5

Findings & recommendations

Simple words, real numbers. Every number below comes from the cleaned data.

Evidence-backed findings with exact figures, plus the gap table and export pack for planning.

🔎 What happened — in simple words

1. Sindh suffered the most by far

About 19 million people in Sindh lived in flooded areas — more than all other provinces combined. Districts like Khairpur, Dadu, Larkana, Badin and Jacobabad each had over a million people exposed.

2. Punjab was second — and partly missed

About 9.8 million people exposed in Punjab, but relief records show only 5.7 million beneficiaries. Many Punjab districts with heavy flooding (Gujranwala, Muzaffargarh, Narowal, Sheikhupura) have no recorded relief rows at all — and Sialkot’s 2 recorded activities report zero people reached.

3. Relief did reach most worst-hit places

Of the 10 worst-hit districts, 9 have large relief operations recorded. Dadu alone shows 3.4 million beneficiaries reached across 7,739 activities.

4. The biggest single gap: Sialkot

1.8 million people exposed in August — the highest of any district — with 2 relief activities on record but zero beneficiaries reported reached. (Its flood was a short August spike that receded fast; still, the records show nobody counted as helped.)

5. Shelter, food and health went widest

These three sectors reached 79–92 districts each. Help for people with disabilities and child protection reached far fewer places.

✅ What should happen next — for citizens

  • If you live in a district flagged with a gap (Gujranwala, Sujawal, Muzaffargarh, Narowal, Sialkot…), ask your local disaster office what relief was delivered — the official records show none, or nobody counted as reached.
  • Before every monsoon, follow official NDMA and PMD advisories; this analysis shows floods can peak in a single month (August 2022).
  • Community groups in Punjab’s riverine districts should register needs early — the data shows relief concentrated in Sindh and Balochistan.
Honest limits: satellite pictures estimate flooding — they are not door-to-door counts. Relief records are reported by agencies themselves and can double-count people. This data cannot tell us about deaths, destroyed houses or livestock — those need NDMA’s records.

🔎 Findings — with figures

F1 · Geographic concentration

91.7% of exposed population sits in two provinces: Sindh 19.09M (59.8%), Punjab 9.82M (30.8%). Balochistan 1.28M, KP 1.66M.

F2 · Provincial relief imbalance

Sindh: 24.78M beneficiaries vs 19.09M exposed (coverage 1.30 — inflated by double counting). Punjab: 5.69M vs 9.82M (coverage 0.58). Punjab is the priority gap at province level.

F3 · 27 districts with no relief rows + 12 with zero beneficiaries reported

27 flood-hit districts have no rows at all in the 5W file, led by Gujranwala (652,954 exposed), Sujawal (635,053), Muzaffargarh (579,197), Narowal (576,082). Another 12 — led by Sialkot (1,813,308 exposed; 2 “Complete” activities, 0 beneficiaries) — have activities on paper but nobody counted as reached. Full list in the gap table below.

F4 · Partial gaps in reached districts

35 more districts have coverage below 0.25 (e.g. Badin: 635,818 reached vs 1,274,371 exposed = 0.50 — partial). Median district coverage is 1.24, but the distribution is heavily skewed.

F5 · Sector skew

Shelter/NFI (92 districts), Food Security & Livelihoods (85), Health (82), WASH (79), Nutrition (77). Disability inclusion and child protection are the thinnest — check district-level sector lists in the export.

F6 · Data-quality note for planners

Beneficiaries (46.6M) exceed exposed population (31.9M) — expected, since one person is counted once per activity. Treat coverage as a relative signal, not a literal percentage of people helped.

✅ Recommendations — for officials & NGOs

  • Verify the 27 zero-row districts (and the 12 with activities but zero beneficiaries reported) against PDMA/NDMA’s own distribution logs — if the 5W is simply missing reports, fix the reporting pipeline; if relief truly didn’t arrive, prioritise them in contingency plans.
  • Rebalance toward Punjab’s riverine belt: pre-position shelter/NFI and WASH stocks for Sialkot, Gujranwala, Narowal, Sheikhupura, Muzaffargarh before the next monsoon.
  • Close the sector gap: bundle disability screening and child-protection into every shelter/food distribution — the thin sectors ride free on the wide ones.
  • Fix 5W hygiene: enforce a district dropdown (239 raw spellings → 133 real districts), mandatory province, numeric validation — the cleaning log shows exactly what breaks.
  • Publish NDMA damage tables as open CSV (deaths, houses, livestock by district) — this analysis had to use satellite proxies because no open district-level damage CSV exists.

⚠️ What this data cannot tell us

  • No deaths, injuries, destroyed houses or livestock — neither file contains them. Those live in NDMA’s sitreps, which are not published as open district-level CSVs.
  • Satellite ≠ ground truth: flood extent comes from radar images; clouds and timing affect it. It estimates exposure, not confirmed damage.
  • Relief records are self-reported by agencies; people helped by several activities are counted several times (that’s why beneficiaries exceed exposed people).
  • Timing mismatch: flood windows are Aug–Nov 2022; relief records run to Dec 2023. A district flooded briefly in August (Sialkot) may have been helped later — or not.
  • District peaks aren’t additive: peaks happened in different weeks, so don’t sum district peaks as a “total affected” — the national figure is UNOSAT’s own 31.25M.
